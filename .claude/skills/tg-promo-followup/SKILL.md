# ThrottleGuard Follow-Up Sequences

Produce full sequences (subject/message + timing), not just one-off messages — that's what distinguishes this from tg-promo-cold-outreach (first touch) and tg-promo-content-angles (organic content).

## Sequence types to build

**1. 14-day trial nurture** (trial starts with no card, full access)
- Day 0: welcome + first CSV upload / first score walkthrough CTA.
- Day 2-3: check-in — did they upload data? If not, remove friction (offer the demo dataset, offer a call).
- Day 7 (midpoint): highlight one specific finding style (e.g., "here's what a CRITICAL score looks like and why") to reinforce value before renewal decision fatigue sets in.
- Day 11-12: pre-expiry nudge — plan comparison (monthly vs. annual), no fake urgency, just a clear "trial ends in 2 days" fact plus what happens to their data/access.
- Day 14/expiry: trial-ended message — clear upgrade path, reassurance their data isn't lost, low-friction upgrade CTA.
- Day 17-21 (if no conversion): soft win-back — ask what held them back, offer a call, don't hard-sell again immediately.

**2. Cold lead re-engagement** (someone downloaded a lead magnet or expressed interest but didn't trial)
- Space touches further apart (days, not hours), each with a different angle from tg-promo-content-angles rather than repeating the same pitch.
- Last touch in a sequence should have a clean "breakup" tone — offer to stop emailing, which often lifts response rate.

**3. Demo/call no-show recovery**
- Same-day: friendly "we missed you, want to reschedule" — no guilt-tripping.
- +2-3 days: one value-forward follow-up (e.g., link to the 17-point checklist) instead of just "still want to talk?"
- +7 days: final reschedule offer, then move to long-term nurture cadence.

**4. Trial-to-paid urgency (post-expiry, engaged but not converted)**
- Focus on what they actually saw in their own data during trial (specific findings) rather than generic reminders — personalization from real usage beats a templated push.

## Tone rules

- Never fabricate urgency (fake countdowns, fake "spots remaining") — see tg-promo-offer for what's actually real.
- Keep copy in the founder's practical, technician-credible voice — not generic SaaS drip-campaign tone.
- Every message needs exactly one CTA — don't stack multiple asks in one email.

## How to run this skill

1. Confirm which sequence type is needed and the channel (email vs. SMS vs. in-app).
2. Ask if there's a real trigger event to personalize around (e.g., "they uploaded data but never came back" vs. "never uploaded anything") — tailor day-0/2 messaging accordingly since the friction point differs.
3. Draft the full sequence with day offsets, subject lines, and body copy — not just bullet summaries — so it's ready to load into an email tool.
4. Flag any step that needs a real data point (specific finding, specific dollar figure) the user must supply rather than a placeholder left unresolved.
